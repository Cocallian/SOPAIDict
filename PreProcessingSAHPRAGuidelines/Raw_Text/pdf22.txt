GUIDELINE ON RESCHEDULING OF A SUBSTANCE OR MEDICINE 
This document is intended to provide guidance to applicants on the scheduling of substances submitted for registration as medicines. 
It represents the South African Health Products Regulatory Authority’s current thinking on the safety, quality and efficacy of medicines 
and takes into account the country’s obligations in terms of international agreements. It is not intended as an exclusive approach. The 
Authority reserves the right to request any additional information to establish the scheduling status of a medicine in keeping with the 
knowledge current at the time of evaluation. Alternative approaches may be used but these should be scientifically and technically 
justified. It is important that applicants adhere to the administrative requirements to avoid delays in the processing and evaluation of 
applications.

1. INTRODUCTION 
This guideline is intended to provide applicants, Holders of Certificates of Registration (HCR) with guidance on 
the criteria applied by the South African Health Products Regulatory Authority (“SAHPRA” or “the Authority”) 
when applying for the rescheduling of a medicine or substance.  
1.1 
Purpose 
This purpose of this document is to provide guidance to applicants on the rescheduling of registered 
medicinal products.  
1.2 
Scope 
This Guideline contains guidance on the suitability of rescheduling medicines or substances. This guideline 
should be read in conjunction with the Medicines and Related Substances Act, 1965 (Act 101 of 1965) 
(herein referred to as the Medicines Act), as amended, and the Regulations issued in terms of this Act; and 
the latest version of the Consolidated Schedules. 
2. LEGAL PROVISION 
In terms of the Medicines Act, a ‘Scheduled substance’ is defined as follows: “means any medicine or other 
substance prescribed by the Minister under section 22A”. Section 22A(2) empowers the Minister of Health “on 
the recommendation of SAHPRA”, to make the Schedules referred to in that section, Section 37A also 
empowers the Minister, again on the recommendation of SAHPRA, to amend the Schedules by notice in the 
Government Gazette.  The official Schedules shall therefore always be those that have been published in the 
Government Gazette or amended by subsequent notice in the Gazette. All medicines are subject to a 
scheduling process on the basis of the substances (active pharmaceutical ingredients) they contain. 
The primary consideration in scheduling a substance is its safety profile, in relation to the therapeutic 
indications for its use. Substances may be listed in one or more of eight Schedules. 
However, substances may also be listed in more than one Schedule, based on dosage form, route of 
administration, strength, indication, dose, duration of treatment or a combination of these factors. Where a 
substance or medicine has been listed in more than one Schedule, the other Schedule(s) in which it is listed is/ 
are indicated in parentheses after the inscription in a particular Schedule. Where exceptions are included in 
an entry, these have been emphasised by printing the word ―except in bold type.

3. GENERAL INFORMATION 
3.1 
The application form for Rescheduling of a Substance or Medicine (GLF-CEM-NS-01A) is to be used 
for applications regarding rescheduling of a substance or medicine in line with the Guideline to the 
Scheduling of Substances and Medicines (SAHPGL-CEM-NS-02). The application form can be 
accessible on SAHPRA website. 
3.2 
An application can be submitted by an applicant who wishes to change the schedule status of a 
registered medicine.  
3.3 
An independent person/s or association may not apply for a rescheduling of a product. However, the 
independent persons or association may bring forth data/ information to the regulator to highlight 
any safety/ efficacy concerns observed by the persons or association for the regulator to assess if a 
feasible action is required. 
NOTE: Safety and efficacy data concerns may be submitted to the regulator independently, where 
applicable. 
3.4 
The same principles as outlined in the Guideline to the Scheduling of Substances and Medicines 
(SAHPGL-CEM-NS-02) would be applied in considering an application for rescheduling. Therefore, to 
justify rescheduling from Schedule 3 or 4 to Schedule 2 or 1, evidence would need to be provided to 
justify use without a prescription. 
If any of the following criteria are met, prescription status would need to be maintained: 
• 
The substance is likely to present a direct or indirect danger to human health, even when used 
correctly, if used without supervision by a medical practitioner or dentist; 
• 
The substance is frequently and to a very wide extent used incorrectly or inappropriately, and 
as a result is likely to present a direct or indirect danger to human health; 
• 
The safety and efficacy of the substance has not yet been adequately described and requires 
further investigation. 
3.5 
Likewise, in order to justify a Schedule 0 status, evidence would need to be provided to show that 
the hazard to health, the risk of misuse and the need for special precautions in handling are minimal, 
and that wider sale would be a convenience to the consumer.

3.6 
A product is normally assigned the highest scheduling status appropriate to any one of the registered 
indications of the product. 
3.7 
If an application is made to reschedule only selected indications of a specific product to a lower 
Schedule a unique name and a separate registration dossier, where applicable, will be required for 
such a product.  
3.8 
It must be noted that dual scheduling on packaging, Professional Information and the Patient 
Information Leaflet is not permitted.  
• 
Only products with indications, strengths, routes of administration and/ or pack sizes 
applicable to the lower schedule will qualify for the lower scheduling status. 
4. SUBMITTING AN APPLICATION  
4.1 
The rescheduling application form Application Form Rescheduling of a Substance or Medicine (GLF-
CEM-NS-01A) accessible on the SAHPRA website, must be submitted to SAHPRA as per section 4.2 
below. 
4.2 
Variation applications for the rescheduling of a registered medicine or substance (Type II) for 
evaluation must be submitted via the SAHPRA Engagement Portal (refer to the SAHPRA latest eCTD 
Specification (SAHPGL-HPA-09) for more information as accessible on the SAHPRA website). 
4.3 
The applicant must ensure that all relevant fields are completed on the application form (GLF-CEM-
NS-01A) and that all supporting documentation is attached. Incomplete applications will be identified 
as deficient, and the evaluation will not progress until deficiencies are addressed. 
4.4 
The prescribed application fee and proof of payment must accompany the application. For the 
current fee payable, refer to the latest fee schedule as published in the Government Gazette and 
published on the SAHPRA website https://www.sahpra.org.za/government-gazette/. 
• 
Payments should be made as per the latest SAHPRA Payment Guideline (SAHPGL-FIN-01).  
4.5 
In the event of a rescheduling application for a product moving to a lower schedule, a unique 
proprietary name and a separate registration dossier is required.    
4.6 
For rescheduling an existing registered product to a lower schedule which is part of an existing 
dossier, a delinking from the master dossier will be required.

5. TIMELINES FOR PROCESSING OF RESCHEDULING APPLICATIONS 
5.1 
Rescheduling: 
5.1.1 
For Rescheduling Type II variation applications made to SAHPRA, the timeline will be 220 
working days.  
5.2 
Letter to the applicant: 
5.2.1 
A correspondence letter will be sent to the applicant regarding the outcome of an 
application, and 
5.2.2 
If an application does not meet the evaluation criteria, a query letter will be sent to the 
applicant stating the deficiencies identified within the application.  
5.3 
Permittable query rounds: 
5.3.1 
The query rounds will be as per General Information guideline (SAHPGL-HPA-07), 
consultations with the applicant can be done where applicable. 
5.3.2 
If the applicant fails to address the queries adequately by the within the permissible query 
rounds, the application will be tabled for rejection. 
• 
The applicant will be required to submit a new application, and the applicable fee shall 
be payable. 
5.3.3 
Each response timeframe should be within 20 working days of receipt and notification of 
recommendation  variations@sahpra.org.za. Applicants may submit a written request for 
extension, 
addressed 
to 
variations@sahpra.org.za 
and 
namesandscheduling.variations@sahpra.org.za . 
5.4 
Scheduling of medicines as approved and gazetted by the Minister of Health  
5.4.1 
Rescheduling recommendations that SAHPRA has approved will be submitted as an 
amendment to the published Schedules, for consideration by the Minister of Health and 
publication in the Government Gazette.
