VARIATIONS GUIDELINE FOR HUMAN AND VETERINARY MEDICINES

veterinary medicines. In addition to this guideline, the South African Health Products Regulatory Authority 
(SAHPRA) reserves the right to request additional information to establish the safety, quality and efficacy of 
a medicine based the knowledge available at the time of evaluation.  SAHPRA is committed to ensuring that 
all registered medicines meet the required quality, safety and efficacy.

1. INTRODUCTION 
 The Variations Guideline includes information on the various categories and codes for variations and  
documents that applicants are  required to  submit.  
The submissions will be evaluated as indicated in the Medicines and Related Substances Act.  
1.1 Purpose 
The purpose of this document is to inform applicants on how to submit applications for variations  to the South 
African Health Products Regulatory Authority (SAHPRA) for Category A and C medicines as well as what to 
expect during assessment of variations . 
1.2 Scope 
The South African Health Products Regulatory Authority (SAHPRA) has compiled a list of  requirements for 
submission of variations to registered products for human and veterinary medicines as well as biologicals. We 
encourage applicants to read this guideline together with the relevant classification and codes (Section 3.4 
below) and SAHPRA guidelines relevant to each Technical Unit as well as the recent relevant guidelines and 
templates listed in Appendix A. 
We have outlined requirements  in this guideline as well as  classification and codes that are aligned to the 
framework of the International Council for Harmonisation: Technical Requirements for Pharmaceuticals for 
Human Use (ICH) and the International Cooperation on Harmonisation: Technical Requirements for 
Registration of Veterinary Medicinal Products (VICH). SAHPRA commits to  global best practices regarding the 
safety, quality and efficacy of health products.  
2. LEGAL PROVISION 
The Medicines and Related Substances Act No. 101 of 1965, as amended, Section 15. (3) (a) (i) - (iii), specifies 
medicines, medical devices and/or In Vitro Diagnostics (IVDs) that require registration, which should comply 
with the prescribed requirements and be safe, efficacious and of good quality. General Regulations of the  
Medicines and Related Substances Act, Regulation 53 (1) and (2) requires that changes to accepted standards 
or specifications of the registered product should be submitted to SAHPRA  for prior approval and the latter  
should be granted before the changes are implemented. 
3. CLASSIFICATION OF VARIATIONS 
3.1 Type IA variations (Notifications): 
Minor changes that have minimal or no impact on quality, safety, or efficacy of the product.

Type IA variations are split into two categories, with differing procedural treatment: 
a) Type IAAN: Minor variations that do not require any prior approval but must be notified by the HCR 
within 12 months following implementation (‘Do and Tell’ procedure). 
b) Type IAIN: Minor variations that must be notified by the HCR at least 47 working days prior to 
implementation. If 47 working days lapse  from the time the notification was submitted, and SAHPRA 
has not provided feedback to   the HCR, the HCR may implement the variation.   
 
Upon submission of notification of Type IAIN to SAHPRA, the Authority may review the Type IAIN notification 
within 47 working days.  
If SAHPRA has not sent the HCR its opinion on the application within 47 working days, the application will be 
deemed implementable. Applicants must satisfy themselves that they meet all the prescribed conditions for 
the change and submit all required documentation with the submission of variations. The variation codes that 
require approval from more than one (1) Technical Unit cannot be implemented until approval is received from 
the respective Technical Unit. 
Note: Failure to meet the specified conditions in the case of notifications of Type IA and minor variations of 
Type IAin or provide all necessary documentation in the application, may lead to the immediate rejection of 
the variation, even if the review time has elapsed. If the rejection letter specifies that the implementation of 
change should be reversed, and the HCR should withdraw the impacted sections of the dossier and revert  to 
the previously approved version within 15 days. 
If any of the conditions are not met or documents are not available, the variation should be classified as a Type 
IB and submitted as such. 
3.2 Type IB variations (minor variations): 
Type IB variations are changes that may have minor impact on the overall safety, efficacy and quality of the 
health product or medicine. Applicants must submit all required documents when they submit application 
for   the variation. 
SAHPRA may review a minor variations of Type IB submission within 87 working days, unless otherwise 
indicated. If SAHPRA has not sent the HCR its opinion on the application within 87 working days, the 
application will be deemed implementable (as applicable depending on the variation code). 
Failure to meet the specified conditions in the case of notifications of minor variations of Type IB or provide 
all necessary documentation in the application, may lead to the immediate rejection of the variation, even if 
the review time has elapsed.  
The timeline for evaluation of Type IB variations is 87 working days. If no response has been received from 
SAHPRA within this time, changes may be implemented, however SAHPRA may still reject, request reversal of 
implementation or request further data after the time has elapsed. Please note that SAHPRA timelines are 
calculated in working days. If the rejection letter specifies that the change should be unimplemented, the HCR 
should revert the impacted sections of the dossier back to the latest approved version by means of a closing 
sequence within 15 days. 
Applications which cannot be implemented after 87 working days include changes which need approval from 
more than one (1) Technical Unit, e.g. change in manufacturer of the finished product, change in primary 
packer or changes in the PI/PIL.

3.3 Type II variations: 
Type II variations are changes that could have major impact on the overall safety, efficacy and quality of the 
health products and/or medicines. The submission for review must be approved by SAHPRA before 
implementations and the documents required for the changes must be submitted together with the 
submission.  
SAHPRA has included the Type II variations classification in this guideline (refer to the relevant classification 
and codes as referenced below) with a view to facilitating timeous review and approval of Type II variations.  
Upon submission of Type II variations to SAHPRA, the Authority will review the Type II submission within 220 
working days for all variations. 
 
NOTE: This guideline lists only the minimum technical data requirements for Type II variations. Applicants are 
advised to include additional data to support the Type II variation if available. Please note that SAHPRA reserves 
the right to request further data if it is necessary. 
Exceptions 
 
The timeline for Certificate Variations applications is 30 working days.  
3.4 Classification Codes: 
For classifications and codes for each of the above-mentioned types of variations, please refer to the 
following classification and codes under respective Technical Unit on https://www.sahpra.org.za/guidelines/: 
a. GLF-HPA-06C: Clinical (CEM) 
b. GLF-HPA-06D: Names and Scheduling (N&S) 
c. GLF-HPA-06E: Inspectorate (INS) 
d. GLF-HPA-06F: Biological medicines (PEM) 
e. GLF-HPA-06H: Certificate Variations (HPA) 
f. GLF-HPA-06I: Quality (PEM) 
g. GLF-HPA-06J: Veterinary 
 
3.5 Where to submit applications 
The SAHPRA Engagement Portal is an online platform designed to facilitate interactions between applicants 
and SAHPRA. The portal enables applicants to submit and track applications, manage their profiles and 
ensure compliance with regulatory requirements. File Transfer Protocol (FTP) details must be set under the 
Organisation Details before attempting to create applications that require eCTD uploads to the SAHPRA FTP 
Server. A request for the FTP User Guide for Files/Dossier Submission and access to the FTP should be sent to 
variationsuploadqueries@sahpra.org.za. When requesting access to the FTP, applicants should include the 
company name and licence number. 
3.6 Submitting variations for duplicates and different strengths 
In the case of  identical variations  being submitted for duplicate products or different strengths of the same 
health products and/or medicines, SAHPRA encourages the applicant to submit a combined dossier rather

3.7 Submitting grouped variations  
3.7.1 Submission of multiple variations in a single application (grouped variation) 
A grouped variation refers to a single application submitted for multiple changes to SAHPRA  for a medicinal 
product. These changes can involve different types of variations, but they must be linked to the same 
marketing authorization and can be grouped together under specific conditions.  While grouping of different 
types of variations (Type IA, Type IB and Type II) is acceptable, variations for different Technical Units should 
not be grouped in a single submission. For information on the acceptable number of variations to be grouped 
in a single submission refer to SAHPRA engagement portal communications.  
There should be a clear link or relationship between the grouped variations.  For example, a Type II variation 
for a new indication might be grouped with Type IAin variations for PI/PIL updates affecting the new 
indication.   
Type I variations may be submitted with Type II variations; however, the Type I variations are not 
implementable until the entire variation submission is approved. Applicants found to have implemented 
Type I variations prior to the approval of Type II variations within the same submission will have all variations 
related to the specific submission rejected. 
For fees applicable to grouped variations refer to Regulations regarding Fees Payable in terms of the 
provisions of the Medicines and Related Substances Act related to quality. 
For clinical variations, each variation in the grouped submission will be charged as per classification code in 
accordance with the type of variation submitted. Each variation must be clearly identified as outlined in GLF-
HPA-06C: Clinical (CEM) and will be charged individually based on its classification. For PV recommendations 
added to the PI, each recommendation must be accompanied by the corresponding classification code and 
payment. A fee will be charged per PV recommendation submitted. 
 
In general, it is recommended that all identified changes related to the product dossier must be submitted as 
individual variations in separate submissions with the applicable fees.  There should be no grouping of 
variations related to the same module in the dossier, unless the changes are related. The exception to this is 
PI/PIL amendments and the annual notifications (Type IA only) for changes made to the dossier in 
compliance with the pharmaceutical quality system of the manufacturer.

3.28 
Work-sharing and extension applications  
3.28.1 Work-sharing 
For information on SAHPRA’s reliance pathways consult the Reliance guideline.  
3.28.2 Extension applications 
Extensions are defined as changes to the registered product that do not fall within the scope of a variation 
and fundamentally alter the product, such as:  
• 
Change in active substance (e.g., new salt, ester, complex). 
• 
Change in strength, pharmaceutical form, or route of administration. 
• 
Other changes specific to veterinary medicinal products to be administered to food-producing 
animals or change or addition of target species. 
 
These applications fundamentally alter the terms of the initial registration and thus cannot be evaluated 
according to a variation’s procedure. Extension applications will typically be accompanied by a new 
registration certificate.   
In terms of procedure, extension applications will be treated as new registrations by SAHPRA.
