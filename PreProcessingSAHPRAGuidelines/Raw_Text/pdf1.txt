SUMMARY 
 
This document provides guidance to applicants who seek to apply for new registration of generics supported 
by Bioequivalence studies. The South African Health Products Regulatory Authority (SAHPRA) can request 
additional information to establish if the health products meet the safety, quality and performance standards. 
While other approaches can be used, the processes should be proven scientifically and technically. SAHPRA is 
committed to ensuring that all registered health products meet the safety, quality and performance 
standards. Applicants must adhere to the administrative requirements to avoid delays in the processing and 
evaluation of applications.

Glossary 
Abbreviation/ Term 
Meaning 
Comparator product 
The comparator product, also referred to as the reference product, is a 
pharmaceutical product with which the multi-source product is intended to be 
interchangeable in clinical practice. Comparator products will normally be the 
innovator products for which efficacy, safety, and quality have been 
established.  
Biobatch  
The batch used to establish bioequivalence or similarity to the comparator 
product as determined in bioequivalence or biowaiver studies, respectively. 
Extension 
Additional products added to the product range are sometimes referred to as 
‘Line Extensions’. This may include addition of an additional Strengths, Dosage 
Forms or New Routes of Administration. 
BMR 
Batch Manufacturing Record 
Innovator product 
The first registered formulation of a new product, generally a patented 
medicine based on original scientific documentation of safety, efficacy, and 
pharmaceutical quality. 
New chemical entity 
An active pharmaceutical ingredient that has never been used in a marketed 
finished dosage form in South Africa.  
Generic medicine/ 
Multisource product 
Pharmaceutically equivalent or pharmaceutically alternative products that may 
or may not be therapeutically equivalent. Multisource pharmaceutical products 
that are therapeutically equivalent are interchangeable. 
Market leader  
In the absence of an RSA innovator, a market leader is described as the 
company selling the largest quantity of a particular product. 
Old medicine  
Unregistered medicines that are on the South African market before 
registration became mandatory with the promulgation of The Medicines and 
Related Substances Control Act, 1965 (Act 101 of 1965). These medicines were 
never formally evaluated by SAHPRA or the former MCC for safety, efficacy, and 
quality.  
PSD 
Particle size distribution.  
Quality control media 
In dissolution studies, QC (quality control) media refers to the specific 
formulation of the dissolution medium used to assess the release of a drug 
substance from a dosage form. 
Q1/Q2 
Generic drugs with certain routes of administration are generally required to 
contain “the same inactive ingredients in the same concentration as the 
reference listed drug (RLD),” this is referred to as Q1/Q2 (Qualitative (Q1) and 
quantitative (Q2)) sameness.

1. INTRODUCTION 
This guideline provides appropriate in vivo and in vitro requirements to assure interchangeability of the multi-
source product. Multisource pharmaceutical products are required to meet safety, quality, and performance 
standards as those applicable to the innovator’s product. In addition, reasonable assurance should be provided 
to ensure that the multisource product is therapeutically equivalent and interchangeable with the product 
they are compared with. 
This guideline should be read in conjunction with the ICH M9, M13, and the Quality and Bioequivalence 
guidelines, and other applicable guidance.   
1.1 Purpose 
To register a new generic medicine in South Africa, the applicant must demonstrate bioequivalence against 
the South African reference product by providing the appropriate in vivo and in vitro studies as detailed in 
the quality and bioequivalence guideline. This guideline provides clarity on the selection of a reference 
product.  
A bioequivalence study using an overseas reference product may be acceptable, provided the applicant can 
demonstrate similarity between the South African and overseas reference products through comparative 
dissolution. 
In line with the adoption of the ICH M13 Guideline, this guideline guides the selection of the reference 
product for conducting bioequivalence studies.  
1.2 Scope 
• 
The choice of comparator product(s) to be used in bioequivalence/ biowaiver applications for 
generic products for registration. 
• 
The choice of a comparator product for comparative dissolution studies. This also includes the 
reference products used in comparative dissolution, where the requirements for comparing the 
foreign reference product with the South African innovator must be met. 
• 
The choice of comparator products for non-solids and semi-solids. 
This guideline does not cover the use of products containing chemical entities or active moieties that are 
not registered in South Africa as reference products to support the safety and efficacy of generic 
applications, for further information on these products, refer to the Clinical Guideline (SAHPGL-CEM-01).

2. LEGAL PROVISION 
Section 15(1)-(3) of the Act states: (1) Every application for the registration of a medicine, medical device or 
IVD shall be submitted to the Chief Executive Officer in the prescribed form and shall be accompanied by-  
(a) the prescribed particulars;  
(b) samples of the relevant medicines;  
(c) where practicable, samples of medical devices or IVDs; and  
(d) the prescribed registration fees.  
(2) As soon as possible after receipt by the Chief Executive Officer of an application contemplated in subsection 
(1), he or she shall inform the applicant in writing that the application is being considered.  
(3) (a) If after consideration of any such application and after any investigation or enquiry which it may consider 
necessary the Authority is satisfied that the medicine, medical device or IVD in question-  
(i) is suitable for the purpose for which it is intended;  
(ii) complies with the prescribed requirements; and  
(iii) is safe, efficacious and of good quality and, in the case of a medical device and IVD, performs as intended, 
the Authority shall issue the applicant with a certificate of registration to that effect.

Reference Products (comparators)  
The reference product should be an innovator product registered by SAHPRA and should preferably be 
procured in South Africa. If more than one such product is available, the market leader should be used as a 
reference and consult SAHPRA for guidance. The applicant should submit evidence to substantiate the 
market leadership claim. 
The following options for selection of the reference product are listed in order of preference: 
i) 
the innovator product registered and procured in South Africa; or 
ii) the innovator product, registered in South Africa, for which a marketing authorisation has been 
granted by one of the health authorities specified in Appendix I. The reference product should be 
purchased from that market; or  
iii) a product from the latest edition of the WHO List of international comparator products, provided 
the product is sourced from the market with which SAHPRA aligns itself.

3.1.2 
Selection of Reference Products for Combination Products: 
Combination products should, in general, be assessed with respect to bioavailability and bioequivalence of 
individual active substances: 
• 
Either single entity products administered concurrently (in the case of clinically justifiable 
combinations), or 
• 
Using an existing combination as the reference, which should be an innovator product registered 
by SAHPRA on safety and efficacy data. 
In the former instance, immediate-release oral dosage forms containing a single API may be used as a 
reference. 
NOTE: The use of unregistered old medicines as reference products is not acceptable. 
3.2 Biowaivers 
In certain instances, the need for a bioequivalence study is waived based on fulfilling the criteria of the so-
called BCS-based biowaiver. In such instances, there is no batch used in bioavailability/ bioequivalence 
study or in clinical testing (biobatch) and by analogy, the batch that is equivalent to a reference product 
based on satisfactory in vitro dissolution data in at least three different pH media is considered to be the 
test batch. 
The reference product used in the biowaiver in vitro studies should be sourced from the South African 
market, in case where the reference product is not available in SA refer to section 4.1 For more information, 
refer to ICH M9.

4. SELECTION OF A REFERENCE PRODUCT FOR COMPARATIVE DISSOLUTION 
In vitro dissolution between the biobatch and the reference product used in the bioequivalence study (BE) 
should be provided in three media (pH 1.2, 4.5 and 6.8) and the quality control (QC) media based on the 
development of that dissolution method. In addition to this, to ensure interchangeability and substitutability, 
if a foreign reference product was used in the BE study, comparative in vitro dissolution should be performed 
between the RSA innovator and the foreign reference product used in the BE study in at least the three media 
(pH 1.2, 4.5 and 6.8) and the quality control (QC) media if applicable. 
4.1 Special cases for dissolution requirements 
In cases where a BE study is conducted with a foreign reference product and the requirement for 
comparability with the RSA innovator cannot be met due to the inaccessibility of the RSA innovator, the 
following mutually exclusive scenarios may apply:  
• 
Scenario 1: The RSA innovator is not available on the market, and the reference product used in 
the BE study and the RSA innovator are from the same marketing authorization holder (MAH). 
Where the BE reference product and the RSA innovator product are from the same MAH, a 
declaration from the Holder of the certificate of registration (HCR) of the RSA innovator product 
should be provided. The declaration should state that the foreign reference product and RSA 
reference product are identical in all respects, including formulation, method of manufacture, and 
manufacturing site.  
• 
Scenario 2: The RSA innovator product is not available on the market, and the leading generic 
product is available on the market. 
In a case where the BE study has been conducted with a foreign reference product, and the RSA 
innovator product is not available for comparative dissolution, the market leader can be used as 
the reference product, and market leadership should be demonstrated by the applicant consult 
SAHPRA for guidance. 
NOTE: For applications where, comparative dissolution is conducted with market leader rather than 
the RSA innovator, it is expected that comparative dissolution will not need to be repeated once 
the SA innovator becomes available on the market. 
Scenario 3: The RSA innovator product and the leading generic are not available on the market. 
In cases where the RSA innovator product and/ or the leading generic product are not accessible, the authority

5. REQUIREMENTS FOR DEMONSTRATING SIMILARITY 
For all applications for generic products, physico-chemical and biopharmaceutics comparison between the 
reference product and the test product should be performed. Where the reference product is not the RSA 
innovator, a comparison of the foreign reference product with the RSA innovator is required. In the case of 
dosage forms not supported by a BE study, at least a comparison of formulation, physico-chemical properties, 
and impurity profiles depending on the formulation should be demonstrated between test product, RSA 
innovator and foreign reference. For requirements for bioavailability for alternative dosage forms, refer to the 
Quality and Bioequivalence guideline (SAHPGL-PEM-02).  
6. REGIONAL REQUIREMENTS 
Refer to the Quality and Bioequivalence Guideline for the requirements for Module 3.2.R.1, 3.2.R.1.2, 3.2.R.1.3, 
and 3.2.R.1.4. Please note the following: 
• 
If the biostudy reference product is expired and cannot be used for comparative in vitro dissolution 
studies, a batch of the reference product procured from the same country and manufacturer as the 
biostudy reference product should be used for dissolution testing. A new biostudy is not required. 
• 
If the biostudy test product (bio-batch) has expired, a representative batch should be selected. The 
selected representative batch should be the same as the bio-batch, i.e., formulation, specification 
for the API, the excipients and the FPP manufacturing site, manufacturing process and controls, 
equipment, and in-vitro performance. Any differences between the bio-batch and the 
representative batch should be clearly stated and justified. A comparative analysis between the 
representative batch and the bio-batch should be provided. In addition, the executed Batch 
Manufacturing records (BMR), multipoint dissolution in the three physiological media at 50 rpm or 
75 rpm, as per ICH guideline, and the Certificate of Analysis (CoA) of the representative batch 
should be provided. Particle size distribution (PSD) characterization of the API lot used in the
