GUIDELINE FOR INDUSTRY E-REPORTING 
This document serves as a guideline to Applicants/ holders of certificates of registration (HCRs) on the method of submission of post-
marketing Adverse Event (AE) reports for human medicines through the industry eReporting VigiFlow module. Thus, this guideline 
must be read in conjunction with the guideline on post-marketing reporting of adverse drug reactions (ADRs) to human medicines in 
South Africa for guidance on what to report to SAHPRA’s Pharmacovigilance (PV) unit as well as the stipulated timelines. The Authority 
reserves the right to request any additional information to establish the safety, quality, and efficacy of a medicine and may make 
amendments to the approved conditions of registration in keeping with the knowledge that is current at the time of consideration of 
safety data.

1.
INTRODUCTION
Industry eReporting is a web-based tool that allows Holders of Certificate of Registration (HCRs) or Applicants
to submit Individual Case Safety Reports (ICSRs) and Adverse Events Following Immunisation (AEFIs) from an
external interface directly to SAHPRA’s National Database in the international standardised format. Industry
eReporting further allows for both upload of the international standards for the electronic transmission of ICSR
(E2B) XML files, in second revision (R2) or third revision (R3) format, or entering reports via manual data entry
by HCRs.
Reporting of ICSRs/AEFIs and monitoring thereof, is a viable means of identifying previously unrecognised, rare 
or serious ICSRs/AEFIs. Every Applicant/ HCR is required to report to the Authority, ICSRs/AEFIs known to them 
involving their marketed medicines in accordance with the requirements of Regulation 40 of the Medicines 
and Related Substances Act, 101 of 1965, as amended. The success of the Authority’s ICSRs/AEFIs reporting 
system depends on the quality, completeness, and accuracy of the information submitted. The Authority 
makes decisions and takes regulatory actions such as updating a Professional Information and Patient 
Information Leaflet, restricting the use of a product in certain populations and withdrawal of a product from 
the South African market, therefore contributing to international data regarding risks and effectiveness of 
medicines, and imparting health product safety knowledge that benefits all South Africans. 
1.1 
Purpose 
This document is intended to guide to HCRs or Applicants on how to register and report “reportable” post-
marketing adverse event reports for human medicines using Industry eReporting VigiFlow module, in 
accordance with SAHPRA requirements specified in the Guideline on Post-marketing reporting of ADRs to 
human medicines in South Africa (SAHPGL-CEM-PV-04). 
In collaboration with Uppsala Monitoring Centre (UMC), SAHPRA has launched an Industry e-Reporting tool, 
already piloted by some countries such as Mexico and Pakistan, to name a few. As of 01 September 2026, 
HCRs/ Applicants are required to submit post-marketing adverse event reports for human medicines 
through a manual data entry module or E2B upload module using the new tool of industry e-Reporting. 
The eReporting for Industry, developed and maintained by the UMC, enables HCRs or Applicants to submit 
reports directly to the National Database, either by manually entering data or uploading E2B XML files: 
•
The manual entry module is an electronic form based on the ICH-E2B (R3) standard, allowing
structured and standardized data entry. This replaces previous hard copy CIOMS form (S01)
submissions.

•
HCRs or Applicants who previously submitted XML reports via email can transition to the E2B

Key features of eReporting for Industry include: 
•
Compatibility with the ICH-E2B standard
•
Preference for structured fields over free-text fields
•
Use of standardized MedDRA and WHODrug terminology
•
Ability to attach relevant additional information in PDF format
•
Immediate transmission of reports to SAHPRA
•
Submission of follow-up reports by uploading and editing the original and previous XML files
(available only for manual data entry; see Section 8 for instructions)
•
Capability to download an electronic acknowledgment receipt (acklog) in XML format
NOTE: 
‒ 
Using the MedDRA and WHODrug Global dictionaries for medical coding in reporting adverse 
events makes it much easier to record and analyze patient data in a consistent and accessible way. 
Therefore, SAHPRA advises HCRs to use both MedDRA and WHODrug while reporting to ensure 
proper terminologies are used in reporting, as our database terminologies are coded based on 
these dictionaries. In the future, SAHPRA will make the use of MedDRA and WHO-Drug mandatory 
for reporting adverse events by all HCRs. 
1.2 
Scope 
This guide covers all reportable post-marketing adverse event reports for human medicines received by 
HCRs or Applicants from various sources, including consumers, healthcare professionals, literature, and 
other media sources, as per the Guideline on Post-marketing reporting of ADRs to human medicines in 
South Africa (SAHPGL-CEM-PV-04). 
Submission of all post-marketing adverse event reports for human medicines includes:

2.
LEGAL PROVISION
This document is intended for HCRs or Applicants. In accordance with Regulation 40 issued in terms of the
Medicines and Related Substances Act, 101 of 1965, as amended.
(1) A person who has applied for the registration of a medicine in terms of section 15 of the Act, a holder
of a certificate of registration in respect of a medicine or scheduled substance, or a holder of a licence
in terms of section 22C (1) (b) must inform the Authority, in the manner and within the timeframe as
determined by the Authority. A holder of certificate of registration (HCR)/Applicant and Licence Holder
must inform the Authority of any:
(a) new or existing quality, safety or effectiveness concerns related to any medicine or scheduled
substance, including but not limited to adverse drug reactions; and
(b) risk management activities associated with paragraph (1).
(2) A person who has applied for registration of a medicine in terms of Section 15 of the Act, a holder of
a certificate of registration in respect of a medicine or scheduled substance, or a holder of a licence in
terms of section 22C (1) (b) must maintain or have access to records of the reports and case reports
referred to in sub regulation (1) above.

3.1 
INITIAL CONSIDERATIONS 
3.1.1 Technical and Operational Requirements 
•
Ensure that the equipment used to load case data is connected to a stable power source or backup
system, and that a reliable high-speed internet connection is maintained. Unstable internet or
power interruptions can result in the loss data if a report has not yet been submitted.
•
Use Google Chrome, Mozilla Firefox, or Microsoft Edge (in that order of preference). Always keep
your browser updated for best results.
•
Do not share access credentials. Protect the confidentiality and integrity of user accounts and
passwords.
•
Log out of eReporting for Industry when it is not actively being used to enter information.
•
Adhere to the provisions outlined in the Terms and Conditions document (Appendix 1).
3.1.2 Transition from Previous Submission Methods 
If your organisation previously submitted reports via email or in hard copy format, follow these instructions 
carefully: 
3.1.2.1 Only new individual case safety reports (ICSRs) and their follow-ups should be submitted 
through eReporting for Industry. 
3.1.2.2 Reports submitted through previous channels must continue to be managed through the 
same method. For example, if the initial and follow-up reports were sent via email, the case 
must also be closed via email, even if you are now using eReporting for Industry. 
3.1.2.3 As of 01 September 2026, HCRs/ Applicants are required to submit all new post-marketing 
adverse event reports for human medicines and their follow-ups through a manual data entry 
module or E2B upload module using the new tool of Industry e-Reporting.

MANAGE LICENCES 
To manage your MedDRA and/or WHODrug licenses, locate the Manage 
licenses option in the upper right menu. 
Once the licenses are activated, you do not need to repeat the activation

If a license expires, eReporting for Industry will block data entry in the

support, please contact the appropriate organisation: MSSO for MedDRA and UMC for WHODrug. 
3.3.1 MedDRA 
To use MedDRA within eReporting for Industry, you must activate your license within the Industry 
eReporting system. Note that this requires obtaining and registering an API key to validate your company’s 
MedDRA license. 
3.3.1.1 MedDRA License Validation Process 
(a) Go to the following external site: https://mid.meddra.org/account/register .
(b) Enter your MedDRA Username and Password, then click Login.
(c) If the login is successful, the page will display your API key.
(d) If unsuccessful, verify your credentials and try again, or contact MSSO.
(e) Copy the API key and log in to eReporting for Industry.
(f) In the upper right menu, click on Manage Licenses.Enter your MedDRA ID in the first
field and the API key in the second field.

3.5.1 NullFlavor (NF) – Explanation and Usage 
NullFlavor (NF) codes are used to indicate why a valid value is not provided in certain fields. These codes 
are only justified when the system requires a value in a mandatory field in order to submit the report to 
SAHPRA. Mandatory fields necessary for submission are highlighted in red by the system. 
If a field contains an NF code and the information is unavailable — but the field is not required for 
submission — you may leave it blank. In such cases, the system will retain the default NF marker 
automatically. Common NF codes include: 
•
Unknown – The information is not known.
•
Asked but unknown – The information was requested but not provided.
•
Not asked – The information was not requested.
•
Masked/concealed – The information is intentionally hidden.
