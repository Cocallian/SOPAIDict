GUIDELINE FOR PHASE 2-4 CLINICAL TRIAL PARTICIPANT TIME, INCONVENIENCE 
AND EXPENSE (TIE) COMPENSATION MODEL 
This document has been prepared to serve as guidance to applicants/sponsors of clinical trials during the use of registered or 
unregistered medicines in approved clinical trials. It represents the South African Health Products Regulatory Authority’s (SAHPRA) 
current thinking on participant reimbursement during clinical trial participation and proposes a minimum compensation that can be 
paid. It is not intended as an exclusive approach and SAHPRA reserves the right to request any additional information and may make 
amendments in keeping with the knowledge that is current at the time.

1.
Inflationary adjustments to recommended amounts
Participant remuneration amounts have been updated to reflect inflationary increases since 
the initial budget approval. The revised rates are intended to maintain equivalent real value 
for participants’ time, travel, and inconvenience. 
2.
Clarifications on existing remuneration components
To ensure consistency across sites and clarity for both participants and site staff, the 
following points have been clarified: 
− 
Remuneration applies to all completed visits and activities 
− 
Remuneration for participants who screen fail 
− 
Payments will be made via EFT, voucher, or cash, and for this reason, amounts will be 
rounded up to facilitate this payment 
3.
Additional Remunerated Activities
Following recent changes in study conduct, several new participant activities now qualify 
for remuneration. These include: 
− 
Reimbursement for telephonic visits 
− 
Remuneration for unscheduled visits 
− 
Participation in any remote data collection activities (for example, paper/digital diary 
completion or device-based monitoring)

1.
BACKGROUND
As per the published National Health Research Ethics Council (NHREC) guidelines (2024) titled “South African 
Ethics in Health Research Guidelines: Principles, Processes and Structures,” Section 3.1.8 states that trial 
participants should be compensated appropriately for their time (T), inconvenience (I), and expenses (E)1.  In 
addition, the Council for International Organisations of Medical Sciences (CIOMS) Guideline states that 
participants may be compensated for inconvenience and time (CIOMS, 2016)2. United Nations Programme on 
HIV/AIDS (UNAIDS)(2012) notes that participants should be compensated for expenses related to participation, 
time, and inconvenience3. 
The South African Health Products Regulatory Authority (SAHPRA) has considered the above and adopted the  
TIE compensation of clinical study participants in South Africa. 
2.
PURPOSE AND SCOPE
This document guides Applicants/Sponsors/Investigators conducting studies and applies to Phase 2 to 4 studies 
conducted in South Africa. Phase 1 studies include a higher risk for participants, hence they should be 
compensated on a different scale following Phase 1/First in human remuneration guidelines. 
3.
LEGAL PROVISION
Guideline is established in terms of Regulation 30 of the Medicines and Related Substances Act, 101 of 1965, 
for the conduct of clinical trials. 
4.
MODEL FOR COMPENSATION FOR CLINICAL STUDY-RELATED VISITS
4.1 
Time spent 
Current minimum wages in South Africa for unskilled labour start from approximately R25-R30 per hour4.  
Factoring in the inconvenience aspect, R50 per hour is recommended4. 
Note: 
4.1.1 
Amounts within a few rands of more cash-manageable numbers may be rounded up, e.g., R99 to 
R100.  This is in order to ease the burden of unnecessary calculation and difficulty in petty-cash 
flow at sites. 
4.1.2 
In general, a standard scheduled visit should not take longer than 3 hours.  More intensive visits 
may, however, require additional time at the site.  This would necessitate a higher “compensation” 
as the participant is now spending an extended period of time away, resulting in them missing the 
majority of their work/school day as opposed to a portion of it.  In such a case, this amount should 
be increased to R50 per hour. 
4.2 
Inconvenience (considered in the light of travel/distance from home to a site) 
Based on rates prescribed by Automobile Association (AA) of South Africa5 and Stellenbosch University6, it 
is recommended that R4.75 per km travelled is paid. The travel cost is broken down into 3 categories (0-
25, 26-50, and >50 km). For example, a participant falling in the first category will receive R237.50 travel 
compensation (R4.75 x 25 km x 2 [return trip]). This should be rounded up to R250 for convenience and to 
make provision for any parking costs. 
Note: 
4.2.1 
It would be ideal for each site Principal Investigator to decide on the radius around the trial site

4.3 
Expenses 
It is recommended that all participants be offered a meal to the value of R100.  If this amount is included 
in the ICF reimbursement total, then the R100 should be removed from the total, documented in the 
relevant documentation and explained to the participant prior to this adjustment being made. Should the 
participant be at the site for longer than 3 hours, an additional meal or money to the value of R100 should 
be made available to them.

4.4 
Additional Inconvenience 
Some studies call for more invasive procedures (e.g., PK sampling) or strenuous procedures (e.g., Stress 
ECGs), which are over and above the standard inconveniences of a study visit. Therefore, a case can be 
made for additional compensation. Other inconveniences which are not always so apparent need to also 
be compensated for, such as an HIV trial in which participants are required to abstain from sexual activity 
and product use for specified periods. These additional procedures must be described in the Protocol and

4.5 
Scheduled telephonic visits 
Telephonic visits are generally conducted in one of the following scenarios: 
•
The protocol specifies that a scheduled visit be conducted telephonically as opposed to onsite, or
•
A volunteer is unable to attend the scheduled visit for a specific reason, and the site is instructed
by the protocol and/or Sponsor to conduct the visit telephonically.
This is still considered to be a trial-related activity, and the participant should thus be remunerated for this 
task. A minimum amount of R150 is recommended. 
4.6 
Home-based/virtual consultation visits 
When permitted by the protocol, a scheduled visit may be conducted either at home or virtually.  The 
participants must be remunerated for time and inconvenience. A minimum of R200 per visit is 
recommended; this could be increased depending on the time and complexity of the visit.  
4.7 
Unscheduled visits 
Unscheduled visits may occur on site, telephonically, virtually, or at home. The same remuneration as 
detailed above applies. 
4.8 
Screen Failures 
A screen failure occurs when a potential volunteer is invited to the site, signs an informed consent, and 
undergoes at least part of a screening visit (such as blood tests, ECG, and/or medical history review), but 
ultimately does not meet the inclusion/exclusion criteria and therefore does not proceed to randomization 
or treatment.  
4.8.1 
When the screening visit has been concluded in its entirety, the full remuneration is due to the 
participant. 
4.8.2 
When the screen failure is identified before all the screening procedures have been completed 
and the patient has not necessarily been allocated a screening number, partial reimbursement for 
the visit is expected. This should be 20-50% of the total reimbursement. 
4.9 
Parent/ Legal guardian/ caregiver 
In studies where the participant requires the presence of a parent/legal guardian/caregiver, the same Time, 
Inconvenience and Expense (TIE) remuneration framework shall apply. Reimbursement should reflect the 
additional time, inconvenience and reasonable expenses incurred by the accompanying individual, while 
remaining aligned with ethical principles that prohibit undue inducement. Where both the participant and 
the parent/legal guardian/caregiver incur separate time and travel burdens, the reimbursement structure 
must transparently account for this and be clearly justified in the study budget for REC approval.  
4.10 In-patients 
In-patients recruited to the trial need not be compensated for distance travelled, but the inconvenience, 
expense, and time beyond in-patient stay need to be taken into consideration.  A minimum of R100 to R250 
per visit is recommended, depending on whether informed consent or other investigations and/or patient-

4.11 Patient diary completion 
The completion of paper and/or electronic diaries between visits is increasingly being incorporated into 
clinical trials. This amounts to an additional trial-related activity and can be inconvenient and burdensome 
for trial participants to complete these diaries. The participant should thus be remunerated for this task. A 
minimum amount of R100 to R150 per completed diary entry is recommended, depending on the extent of 
the trial-related procedures (e.g. dosing, temperature measurements, etc.) that need to be captured.  
In addition, when using electronic diaries, it must be highlighted that most people in South Africa do not 
have access to free wi-fi. Free wi-fi and/or sufficient airtime should be made available to all participants to 
complete the necessary trial-related activities. Alternatively, sponsors can provide devices that have data 
preloaded. 
4.12 Electronic Clinical Outcome Assessment (eCOA), including the use of tablets, 
smartphones, wearables, and other such devices 
As for 4.11 above, participants need to be remunerated appropriately for this task. In addition, sufficient 
airtime needs to be made available to all participants, and/or a device with pre-loaded data needs to be 
provided by the Sponsor.
