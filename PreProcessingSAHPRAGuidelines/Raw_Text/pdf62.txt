GUIDELINE FOR SUBMISSION OF EXTENSION APPLICATIONS 
   
This guideline is intended to provide guidance to applicants who intend to submit extension applications for registration. 
It represents the South African Health Products Regulatory Authority’s (SAHPRA) current position on the safety, quality 
and efficacy of these medicines.  
  
It is not intended as an exclusive approach; alternative approaches may be used but these should be scientifically and 
technically justified. SAHPRA reserves the right to request any additional information to establish the safety, efficacy, 
and quality of a medicine in keeping with the knowledge current at the time of evaluation. SAHPRA is committed to 
ensure that all registered medicines will be of the required quality, safety and efficacy. It is important that applicants 
adhere to the administrative requirements to avoid delays in the processing and evaluation of applications.

1. 
INTRODUCTION  
 
This guideline outlines the information required for the registration of extension applications. The information 
submitted will be evaluated in terms of the provisions of the Medicines and Related Substances Act, 1965 (Act 
101 of 1965) (hereafter “the Act”). 
Extension applications refer to applications for the registration of a medicine that is related to an already 
registered medicine. These applications may be submitted by the same Holder of the Certificate of Registration 
(HCR) and are referred to as line extension applications. 
A line extension application is an application where for instance the pharmaceutical form and/or strength 
differs from one or more other medicines for which the applicant already holds a registration certification. For 
example, if an HCR holds a registration for paracetamol 500 mg tablets and applies for paracetamol 250 mg 
syrup, this will be classified as a line extension. 
Applications submitted by a different HCR will be considered as extension applications, provided they fall 
within the categories outlined below. 
Extension applications fall within these two main categories:   
     1. Changes to the active substance(s)  
     2. Changes to strength, pharmaceutical form and route of administration  
These changes may be associated with changes related to therapeutic indication(s) and/or posology, assuming 
that the proposed population is included in the indication. These extension applications will be regarded as 
generics with clinical data. Applicants should refer to the Clinical Guideline for more detail. 
It is a legal requirement that data submitted for evaluation should substantiate all claims and should meet 
technical requirements of quality, safety and efficacy of the product for the purposes for which it is intended. 
The guideline is meant to guide the applicant in meeting the requirements of the Act. It is acknowledged, 
however, that in some instances scientific developments may dictate alternative approaches. When a 
deviation from a guideline is decided on, a detailed motivation giving the reason(s) for the deviation and 
justification for the alternative approach should be included in the expert report submitted with the 
application. 
1.1 
Purpose 
The aim of this document is to provide guidance to applicants on the appropriate data for extension 
applications for the registration of medicines for human use.  
 
1.2 
Scope 
The guideline is applicable to categories A: Human Medicines, as defined in the Medicines and Related 
Substances Act (Act 101 of 1965). In addition to this guideline, SAHPRA reserves the right to request any

Requirements for Therapeutic equivalence 
(1) A medicine is considered therapeutically equivalent to another medicine if both 
medicines - 
(a) are - 
(i) pharmaceutically equivalent, in that they contain the same amount of active substances in the same dosage 
form, meet the same or comparable standards and are intended to be administered by the same 
route; or 
(ii) pharmaceutical alternatives, in that they contain the same Active Pharmaceutical Ingredient (API) but differ 
either in chemical form of that API or in the dosage form or strength; and 
(b) after administration in the same molar dose, their effects with respect to both efficacy and safety are 
essentially the same. 
(2) Therapeutic equivalence is determined from comparative bioavailability, pharmacodynamic, clinical 
or in vitro studies which meet the requirements and accepted criteria for bioequivalence as determined by the 
Authority. 
2. 
LEGAL PROVISION 
The registration of a medicine in South Africa is governed by the provisions and requirements of the Medicines 
and Related Substances Act, 1965 (Act No. 101 of 1965), and the regulations and guidelines published in terms 
thereof.  
3. 
EXTENSIONS 
Extension applications refer to medicines based on a registered API. These medicines typically contain the 
same active substances as the registered reference medicine and generally fall within the same therapeutic 
class, and they may serve as therapeutic alternatives. 
3.1 
Types of Extensions 
Categorization and definitions of the common types of extensions and how they relate to the registered 
medicine. 
3.1.1 Changes to the active substances 
The following changes to the active substance may be regarded as extension applications:  
I. 
Replacement of the active substance(s) by a different salt/ester complex/derivative (with the same 
therapeutic API) where the efficacy/safety characteristics are not significantly different. Example: 
replacing hydrochloride salt with a citrate salt of the same active substance. 
II. 
Replacement by a different isomer, a different mixture of isomers, of a mixture by an isolated isomer

where the efficacy/safety characteristics are not significantly different. Example: substituting a 
racemic mixture with a single enantiomer. 
Such changes must retain the same active substance(s) and demonstrate that the altered compound does not 
significantly impact the efficacy or safety profile of the product. 
3.1.2 Changes to strength, pharmaceutical form and route of administration 
The following applies to extension applications related to strength, dosage form, and route of administration.  
 
i. 
Change of bioavailability 
ii. 
Change of pharmacokinetics (e.g., change in rate of release) 
iii. 
Change or addition of a new strength/potency 
iv. 
Change or addition of a new pharmaceutical form 
v. 
Change or addition of a new route of administration  
3.1.2.1    Changes to bioavailability and pharmacokinetics: 
Changes to the strength, pharmaceutical form or route of administration may significantly impact a medicine’s 
bioavailability and PK characteristics. For instance, altering the release rates (e.g., from immediate release to 
modified release). These changes must be evaluated through a risk-based approach, with appropriate clinical 
and/or bioequivalence data submitted. 
3.1.2.2  
Changes to strength 
Changes to strength refer to modifications in the amount of active substance per unit dose or concentration.  
The quantitative composition in terms of active substance represents the strength. The concept of strength 
and the concept of concentration are inherently linked. Strength represents the amount of active substance in 
the pharmaceutical form, which can be defined per unit dose or as a concentration. The concentration can be 
stated per unit of mass (e.g. 250 mg/g) or per unit of volume (e.g. 2 mg/mL) or in percentage (e.g. 5%) for 
medicines for which the strength has traditionally been expressed in such way. An extension application may 
have the same active substance but different strength from the registered reference product.  
3.1.2.3  
Changes to the pharmaceutical form and route of administration: 
A pharmaceutical form is used to describe the manufactured product (i.e. the item as presented in the 
packaging by the manufacturer) or the pharmaceutical product (i.e. the item as intended to be administered 
to the patient, after any necessary transformation has been carried out). Changes to the pharmaceutical form 
thus, refers to modifications of the physical form of the medicine such as tablets, capsules, suspensions, or 
other dosage forms. For example, a registered 500 mg tablet vs. a new application for a 500 mg oral 
suspension. 
The route of administration refers to the path by which the medicine taken into or applied to the body to 
achieve its intended effect. A change to the pharmaceutical form and/or route of administration may have a 
significant impact on the pharmacokinetic (PK) and pharmacodynamic (PD) profile of the medicine. Example: 
changing from oral to parenteral.

An extension application may thus have the same active substance as the registered medicine but differ in 
dosage form or formulation. Such extensions may differ from the registered medicine in administration and/or 
dosage. 
4. 
Combination Medicines 
For new combination medicines, it is necessary to take three factors into account: 
• 
The safety and efficacy of the individual active substances. 
• 
The safety and efficacy of the simultaneous use of the individual active substances (multi-
component regimen); and the medical and scientific rationale should support the simultaneous 
use of more than one active substance. 
• 
The possible chemical and physical interactions between active substances derived from a multi-
component regimen when they are formulated into an FDC product. 
• 
The APIs have suitable PK and Physiochemical properties. 
These applications can be considered as extension applications, but the type of application will be a generic 
with clinical data or new chemical entity (NCE) depending on whether all APIs are registered or not, changes 
to the active substance(s) and changes to the strength, pharmaceutical form and route of administration. 
 
N.B. Applications for extension in which the API of the original/innovator is not registered with SAHPRA will be 
regarded as NCEs and innovators, not an extension.  
4.1

In general, the clinical data from the original/innovator product may be provided by the applicant in support 
of the extension application. However, the data may not be sufficient to justify the extension applications, 
resulting in additional data being required to support the submission.
