This guideline document deals with compiling and submission of sealed source monthly reports. It also 
deals with cradle-to-grave racking process to account for all sealed radioactive sources that are used 
in South Africa. The tracking covers the manufacture, storage, use, transport, disposal (sale, transfer 
or discarding) of sources in South Africa, as well as import and export. 
As part of this process, distributors and transporters of radioactive sources are required to submit 
reports and supporting documents monthly. The monthly report summarises all transactions 
involving radionuclides during the relevant month.

1. INTRODUCTION 
 
The regulation published under Government Notice R247 in Government Gazette 14596 of 26 February 1993 
a sealed source is “a Group IV hazardous substance that is firmly bonded within solid inactive material or sealed 
in an inactive capsule of sufficient mechanical strength to exclude the possibility of contact with such substance 
and of the dispersion thereof into the environment under foreseeable conditions of use and wear; but this 
definition shall not apply where such bonding or encapsulation is solely for the purpose of storage, transport 
or disposal”. 
 
A sealed source is a radioactive material (Group IV hazardous substance) that is either: 
1. Permanently bonded within solid, non-reactive material, or 
2. Encased in a durable, inactive capsule 
This containment ensures: 
• 
No direct contact with the radioactive material, 
• 
No environmental contamination, and 
• 
Safety during normal use and expected wear.

1.1 
Purpose 
 
These guidelines provide clear instructions for monthly report submissions under the relevant legislation. 
They outlines the correct procedures and reporting requirements to help ensure accurate and compliant 
submissions from all users. 
 
1.2 
Scope 
 
These guidelines apply to all distributors and transporters of sealed sources who supply end users of sealed 
sources. It covers sealed sources inventory management, movement tracking, disposal records, andusage. 
The requirements outlined apply to authority holders who distribute and transport sealed sources.

3. PROCEDURES FOR DISTRIBUTORS AND  MANUFACTURERS 
Distributors and manufacturers should use the GLF-RDN-RN-14A form to account for transactions involving 
sealed sources on a monthly basis. The GLF-RDN-RN-14A form should be completed, signed and date, and be 
emailed to: radionuclldes@sahpra.org.zaIf you are working electronically, you can add extra lines to the form 
as needed, then convert the completed form to PDF format, sign and submit it (Please do not omit columns or 
re-organise the form). It is helpful to us if you submit the electronic MS Word or MS Excel document along with 
the PDF version. 
• 
All columns must be completed; however, a container serial number is not required where the 
source is packaged in an unnumbered lead pot or similar protective holder. 
• 
Under Activity, use K, M, G or T to indicate KBq, MBq, GBq or TBq respectively. 
The GLF-RDN-RN-14A form specifies the supporting documentation that must be included with the monthly 
report. 
• 
The source calibration certificates must be copies of the manufacturer’s original certificates, specifying 
the radionuclide involved, the serial number and the activity on a given date.

3.1 
Sources brought forward from previous months 
The figure you enter in Section 3.1 must correspond to the figure you entered in Section 3.5 of the previous 
month’s report, because the number of sources in stock at the beginning of any month will always be the 
same as the number in stock at the end of the previous month. 
 
Write this figure in the shaded box marked N1 (for number of sources in Section 3.1). 
 
3.2 
Sources acquired during this month 
There are three ways in which distributors generally acquire sources: 
(a) purchase from a supplier, manufacturer or other local authority holder. 
 
(b) accept from an authority holder for delivery to NECSA for final disposal; or 
 
(c) import from another country. 
 
 
Note that Section 3.2 is divided into three parts, corresponding to the above three ways of acquiring sources. 
 
 
(d) For purchase from local producers/suppliers, attach a delivery note and source certificate as described 
earlier, and check that you have a copy of each buyer’s current authority to possess and use the sources. 
(e) For sources accepted from a local authority holder (client) for final disposal (including exchange), 
attach the following documents: 
• 
The client’s letter W02, received from SAHPRA after submission of the GLF-RDN-RN-14A. This 
letter gives the authority holder permission to send the sources to NECSA for final disposal. 
• 
The client’s source list, with ticks indicating the sources to be disposed of. 
• 
A delivery note proving that the sources were collected from the client. 
(f) For imports, include source certificates, waybills, and, if possible, copies of each supplier’s authority 
or licence (from their regulator) to export the sources.

3.3 
Sources disposed of during this month 
There are three ways in which distributors generally dispose of sources: 
(a) sale to local authority holders; 
 
(b) delivery to NECSA for final disposal; or 
 
(c) export (for sale to a foreign buyer or return to a supplier/manufacturer). 
 
 
Section 3.3 is divided into three parts corresponding to these three routes of disposal. 
(a) For sales to local authority holders, attach the delivery note and check that you have a copy of the 
buyer’s current authority to possess/use the sources. 
(b) For final disposals, provide proof that NECSA received the specified sources: 
 
1. a copy of the waybill or delivery note detailing the sources; and 
 
2. confirmation of receipt by the Nuclear Liability Management (NLM) department at NECSA.

3.4 
Sources in stock at month-end 
Sources will be in stock if they were: 
• 
‘Brought forward’ from previous months (Section 3.1), or 
• 
Acquired during the month in question (Section 3.2) 
and were not disposed of (Section 3.3) during the month under consideration. 
• 
Sources that are in transit to other authority holders will be counted as “in stock” until proof 
of delivery (POD) is provided. 
• 
Similarly, sources that are on an export authority but have not yet passed through Customs 
will still be counted as being “in stock”. 
Count the sources in your stock list and fill in the number in the shaded box marked N4 (for number of 
sources in Section 3.4).

3.5 
Check sum of month-end stock 
 
This is a simple sum using the totals in the shaded boxes at the end of each section: 
The number of sources brought forward (Section 3.1) 
PLUS 
The number of sources acquired (Section 3.2) during the month 
MINUS 
The number of sources sold, disposed of, or exported (Section 3.3) 
EQUALS 
The number of sources in stock at the end of the month 
 
The answer must correspond with N4, the number of sources listed in Section 3.4. 
Remember to enter your name and contact details and sign your form.
