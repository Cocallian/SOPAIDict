GUIDELINE ON MINIMUM REQUIREMENTS FOR INSPECTING 
GROUP III AND GROUP IV HAZARDOUS SUBSTANCES 
This guideline delineates the mandatory regulatory framework of the Radiation Control Inspectorate sub-unit including 
the inspection requirements to all Group III and Group IV Hazardous Substances, in terms of the Hazardous Substances 
Act No. 15 of 1973 and Regulations R1332 and R247.  
 
The guideline further provides directions to all holders of Group III and Group IV Hazardous Substances on maintaining 
compliance with the applicable provisions of the Act and relevant regulations. It sets out the minimum requirements and 
standard to be adhered to during routine, investigative, and enforcement inspections.

1. 
INTRODUCTION 
The South African Health Products Regulatory Authority (SAHPRA) is mandated, in terms of the Hazardous 
Substances Act No. 15 of 1973 as amended, to regulate, control, and monitor the production, acquisition, 
importation, exportation, possession, disposal, use, operation, letting, sale, and installation of Group III and 
Group IV hazardous substances, in order to protect workers, the public, and the environment from radiation-
related harm. 
Group III and Group IV hazardous substances outline varying levels of radiological risk depending on the nature 
of the radioactive material, the activity involved, and the way they are used. Effective inspection of facilities 
and practices involving these substances through a graded regulatory approach is therefore essential to ensure 
compliance with regulatory requirements and adherence to radiation safety principles. 
This guideline provides a standardised framework, consistent with SAHPRA's regulatory approach, promoting 
uniform, transparent, and effective inspections of Group III and Group IV hazardous substances. 
1.1 Purpose 
The purpose of this guideline is to: 
• 
Define the minimum requirements for conducting inspections of facilities and activities involving 
Group III and Group IV hazardous substances. 
• 
Provide guidance to SAHPRA inspectors on the inspection process, focus areas, and compliance 
expectations. 
• 
Promote a risk-based and consistent inspection approach aligned with SAHPRA’s Radiation 
Control framework. 
• 
The goal is to ensure the licence or authority holders understand the entire process and are fully 
prepared to meet all requirements.  
1.2 Scope 
This guideline applies to: 
• 
All facilities, institutions, and users authorized or required to be authorized to possess, use, 
store, transport, or dispose of Group III and Group IV hazardous substances. 
The guideline covers routine, announced, unannounced, follow-up, and compliance inspections but does

2. 
LEGAL PROVISION 
This guideline is issued in terms of the following legal instruments: 
• 
Hazardous Substances Act No. 15 of 1973, as amended, 
• 
Regulations promulgated under the Hazardous Substances Act relating to Group III and Group IV 
hazardous substances, 
• 
Conditions of licencing/ authorization issued by SAHPRA, and 
• 
Applicable national standards, codes of practice, and international guidance adopted or recognized by 
SAHPRA. 
Compliance with this guideline does not exempt any License or Authority Holder from complying with the Act, 
regulations, or specific licensing or authorization conditions. Where there is a conflict between this guideline 
and the Act, regulations, licensing or authorization conditions, the latter shall prevail. 
3. 
RADIATION CONTROL INSPECTION PROCESSES 
Radiation Control inspections shall be conducted by SAHPRA Radiation Inspector appointed in accordance with 
Hazardous Substance Act, section 8 and shall follow a graded, risk-informed approach. 
3.1 Pre-Inspection Activities  
• 
Review of the licence or written authority, scope, and conditions. 
• 
Review of previous inspection findings, non-compliances, and corrective actions. 
• 
Assessment of the radiological risk profile, type of practice, and compliance history. 
• 
Determination of inspection frequency and inspection scope based on the graded approach. 
3.2 On-Site Inspection  
• 
Opening meeting with management and the Radiation Protection Officer (RPO) or responsible 
person. 
• 
Verification of compliance with legislative and licencing/ authorization requirements.

• 
Inspection of facilities, equipment, storage areas, and safety systems. 
• 
Review of documentation, records, and training evidence. 
• 
Interviews with relevant staff involved in the use or supervision of hazardous substances. 
• 
Ensuring controlled entry and display of emergency contact persons and contact details. 
3.3 Post-Inspection Activity  
• 
Closing meeting to communicate preliminary findings. 
• 
Classification of inspection findings (e.g. critical/ major, intermediate, minor, observations). 
• 
Issuing of an inspection report specifying corrective actions and timelines. 
• 
Follow-up inspections or enforcement actions, where warranted. 
4. 
REQUIREMENTS FOR INSPECTION OF GROUP III HAZARDOUS SUBSTANCES  
Inspections of Group III hazardous substances shall assess compliance with the following minimum 
requirements:  
4.1 Licensing and Governance  
• 
Availability of Hazardous Substance Act, 15 of 1973, on site. 
• 
Availability of Regulations R1302 and 1332, on site. 
• 
Valid SAHPRA license covering the specific substances, activities, and locations. 
• 
Appointment and competence of a Radiation Protection Officer or Responsible Person. 
• 
Appointment of Medical Physicist where applicable. 
• 
Availability of a documented radiation protection programme and operating procedures. 
• 
Procedures for incident management and reporting to SAHPRA. 
4.2 Radiation Protection and Safety 
• 
Application of the principles of (ALARA), justification, optimisation and dose limitation.

• 
Adequate control of occupational and public exposure. 
• 
Use of personal dosimetry.  
• 
Workplace monitoring, where applicable. 
4.3 Facilities, Storage and Security 
• 
Appropriate shielding. 
• 
Adequate access control, and signage. 
• 
Measures to prevent unauthorised use (in case of mobile units: loss or theft). 
4.4 Equipment and Instrumentation 
• 
Availability of suitable radiation monitoring instruments. 
• 
Evidence of calibration. 
• 
 Quality Assurance (QA) programme. 
• 
 Maintenance and functionality of equipment. 
4.5 Records, Incidents, and Reporting 
• 
Records of acquisition, use, transfer, and quality control testing.  
• 
Procedures for incident management and reporting to SAHPRA. 
• 
Occupational dose monitoring records. 
• 
Current proof of professional registration of all practitioners involved. 
4.6 Inspection Frequency (Graded Approach – Group III)  
Inspection frequency shall be determined based on risk, compliance history, and complexity of the practice, 
and shall typically be: 
• 
High-risk Group III practices or poor compliance history: at least once every 12 months. 
• 
Moderate-risk Group III practices with satisfactory compliance: at least once every 24 months. 
• 
Low-risk Group III practices with demonstrated sustained compliance: at least once every 36

5. 
REQUIREMENTS FOR INSPECTION OF GROUP IV HAZARDOUS SUBSTANCES  
Inspections of Group IV hazardous substances shall focus on the following minimum requirements:  
5.1 Authorization and Governance   
• 
Renewed and current Authority detailing specific substances, activities, locations and covering 
prescribed conditions. 
• 
Confirmation that the activity falls within the applicable authorization or exemption framework. 
• 
Appointment and competence of a Radiation Protection Officers (RPO and ARPO). 
• 
Appointment of Medical Physicists where required. 
• 
Awareness of Hazardous Substances Act No. 15 of 1973 and Regulations related to Group IV 
Hazardous Substances, R246 and R247. 
• 
Availability of a documented radiation protection programme and operating procedures. 
• 
Procedures for incident management and reporting to SAHPRA. 
• 
Annually updated practice Internal Rules. 
• 
Evidence of Annual Returns submitted to Radiation Control: radionuclides sub-unit. 
• 
Register or documentation for disposal of radioactive waste.  
• 
Radioactive source register.  
5.2 Safe Handling and Use   
• 
Implementation of basic radiation safety practices. 
• 
Adequate supervision and user training appropriate to the practice. 
• 
Implementation of Code of Practice regarding the safe handling and use of Group IV Hazardous 
Substances.

5.3 Storage and Control 
• 
Appropriate shielding, storage and containment of radioactive substances. 
• 
Measures to prevent loss, damage, or unauthorised access. 
• 
Monthly inventory of all radioactive sources. 
5.4 Radiation Awareness and Training  
• 
Evidence of basic radiation safety awareness training for users. 
• 
Availability of written safety instructions. 
• 
Awareness by the RPO and responsible person of regulatory obligations. 
5.5 Equipment and Instrumentation   
• 
Availability of suitable radiation monitoring instruments (survey meters, if used on patients, they 
must be calibrated). 
• 
Personal radiation monitoring dosimeters. 
• 
Radioactive source register (Logbooks). 
• 
In case of medical and research facilities: Hot laboratory availability and functioning (extractor 
fans, fume hood, laminar flow cabinet, continuous flooring, lead-lined work areas, etc). 
• 
Evidence of Quality Assurance (QA) program, maintenance, and functionality of equipment. 
5.6 Records and Incident Management  
• 
Basic inventory and leak tests records. 
• 
Occupational dose monitoring records. 
• 
Proof of professional registration of practitioners 
• 
Reporting and documentation of incidents or abnormal events, where applicable. 
5.7 Inspection Frequency (Graded Approach – Group IV)  
Inspection frequency shall be determined based on risk, compliance history, and complexity of the practice,
