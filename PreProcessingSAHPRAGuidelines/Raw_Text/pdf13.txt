These guidelines provide recommendations to manufacturers who wish to submit Plasma Master Files in support of the 
quality of the starting materials for Plasma-Derived Medicinal Products (PDMP). These apply to applications for the 
registration or variations of PDMP. These guidelines emphasize   South African Health Products Regulatory Authority’s 
(SAHPRA) commitment towards the safety, efficacy, and quality of medicines.  SAHPRA reserves the right to request any 
additional information to establish the safety, efficacy, and quality of a medicine in keeping with the latest knowledge at 
the time of evaluation. Alternative approaches may be used but the knowledge that would have been utilized to inform 
a decision should be scientifically and technically justified. SAHPRA is committed to ensure that all registered medicines 
will be of the required safety, efficacy, and quality. It is important that applicants adhere to the administrative 
requirements to avoid delays in the processing and evaluation of applications. 
 
 
These guidelines and application forms are also available on www.sahpra.org.za

1. INTRODUCTION AND PRINCIPLES OF A PMF 
 
The starting material of plasma-derived medicinal blood products should comply with Ph. Eur. and cGMP 
(Directive 2001/83/EC EU), WHO Requirements for the collection, processing and quality control of blood, 
blood components and plasma derivatives, Annex 2, TRS No 840, and WHO recommendations for the 
production, control and regulation of human plasma for fractionation, Annex 4, TRS No 941). 
Note: the applicant for the PMF may decide to comply with either the EU or WHO directives. 
Both have similar standards and are acceptable. Suppliers in Europe do not in all cases comply 
solely with EU requirements. 
The SAHPRA Guidelines on the Requirements of a Plasma Master File (PMF) lay down the specific 
requirements for the plasma, plasma pools and their constituents and excipients, which are part of the 
starting material of the medicinal products for which the PMF is applicable. This information applies to the 
selection of donors, and the collection and testing of the blood and blood components. In addition, every 
blood establishment or plasma collection centre must be approved and inspected annually according to GMP 
by a Competent Authority. Approval is required for processing, storage and transport of blood and blood 
components, including plasma, intended for the manufacturing of medicinal products. 
Cryoprecipitate or other intermediates are not part of the PMF certification. The manufacturing process of 
the medicinal products, starting from the pooled plasma is not part of the PMF dossier. The PMF is a stand- 
alone document which is separate from the dossier for marketing authorisation. The dossier for marketing 
authorisation for each individual medicinal product should provide the information on the requirements of 
the human plasma that is used as starting material. Applicants that use the PMF certification system must 
identify and make reference to the PMF(s) in the dossier of each medicinal product. 
The characteristics of plasma collected in countries or various regions of countries may differ. Such 
differences may be related to the prevalence or incidence of transfusion-transmissible diseases in the 
country or regions within the respective countries. The applicant should identify such differences and relate 
these to the possible increased risk posed by using plasma from such countries or regions. 
Annual update 
 
The PMF information shall be updated and re-submitted for approval on an annual basis. This annual update 
should include all changes and updated information since the last approval (initial or annual). The update 
should include a list for which it was retrospectively found there were indications that a donation 
contributing to a plasma pool was infected with HIV or hepatitis A, B or C or another transfusion- 
transmissible agent. It should also include the number of positive donations that have been identified per

The plasma master file is a stand-alone document and describes the following: 
a) Plasma source and collection 
b) Epidemiological data on blood transmissible infections 
c) Characteristics of donations and selection/exclusion criteria 
d) Testing of blood/plasma donations and pools for infectious agents 
e) Plasma quality and safety 
f) Conditions of storage and transport of plasma; and 
g) A copy of the plasma specification and plasma pool batch analysis data. 
 
1.1 Purpose 
 
Applications for human plasma-derived medicinal products shall be evaluated based on the products’ quality, 
safety and efficacy. This guideline outlines the requirements for the Plasma Master File (PMF) and the 
specific documentation required to support registration of these products. 
1.2 Scope 
These guidelines include submissions of Plasma Master files, whereby the plasma is used as a component or 
intermediates derived for producing plasma-derived medicinal products (PDMPs). The PMF guidelines will 
apply exclusively to Category A medicines manufactured using plasma or intermediates of plasma. These 
guidelines do not refer to Plasma Master Files that have valid certification of the PMF as per the European 
Commission 
 
2. LEGAL PROVISION 
 
The legal provision for the registration of medicines, medical devices, including In Vitro Diagnostics (IVDs), is 
included in the Medicines and Related Substances Act No. 101 of 1965 as amended and the gazetted 
General Regulations. 
Specific reference is made to Section 15(1) - (10) of the Act and Section 16(1) - (10) of General Regulations.

3. GENERAL INFORMATION 
 
3.1 Plasma-derived products’ list 
 
The plasma - derived products list provides   an annex plasma-derived medicinal products which are relevant 
to the PMF. In addition, it includes investigational products, cryoprecipitate and intermediates derived from 
the plasma of the PMF. And it states whether each of the listed medicinal products has been granted 
marketing authorisation or is in the process of being granted such authorisation by the relevant Competent 
Regulatory Authority(ies). Lastly, it includes the SAHPRA product registration number where applicable. 
 
3.2 Overview of safety strategy 
 
A critical overview of the overall strategy to ensure the safety of the plasma included in the PMF should be 
provided. This evaluation should include for each collection site the epidemiological data on blood 
transmissible infections known for the donor population, viral load limits for plasma pools and normal size 
of plasma pool., The system of donor selection criteria, , criteria for the use of recovered plasma (from 
whole blood donation) and/or source plasma (from plasmapheresis), measures that reduce the risk of 
(v)CJD, screening of donations, minipool strategy if relevant, the testing of the plasma pools, inventory hold, 
and “look-back” procedures should be described.  
The information on centres where blood or plasma is collected, including mobile clinics, must be 
summarised. The applicant must also provide information on the test laboratories, the in vitro diagnostic 
devices used, and the sensitivity of the assays performed. 
The estimated residual risk of undetected viraemic donations that may enter the production pool should be 
described and discussed (WHO Guidelines on estimation of residual risk of HIV, HBV or HCV infections via 
cellular blood components and plasma, Annex 4, TRS No 1004).It should be demonstrated how the facility’s 
safety strategy ensures that the measures taken throughout the collection, processing, testing, storage and 
transport of the plasma robustly ensures that the plasma pool is safe. 
3.2.1 General logistics 
 
A flow-chart, describing the complete supply-chain for plasma from collection to the manufacturing process 
of the plasma pool, must be provided. This should include all establishments concerned in the collection, 
testing, processing, storage and transport of blood or plasma and their relationship. The transport chain 
should include details of international transport and customs.

4. INFORMATION ON STARTING MATERIALS 
 
The quality and safety of plasma-derived medicinal products depend on the source of the plasma and the 
manufacturing process of the medicinal product. The collection, testing, processing, storage and transport of 
human plasma are thus major factors in the quality assurance of the manufacturing process of plasma-
derived medicinal products. Blood establishments and centres should fulfil the requirements, be inspected 
and approved according to the relevant directives of the Competent Regulatory Authority. 
4.1 Plasma origin 
4.1.1 Information on centres or establishments in which blood/plasma collection is carried out, including 
inspection and approval, and epidemiological data on blood transmissible infections. Provide, in tabular 
format, a complete list of names and addresses of blood establishments and centres from which plasma is 
collected. If mobile or temporary equipped centres are used, the relationship with their respective blood 
establishments should be provided. There should be a statement that these mobile and temporary centres 
operate under the same quality system as the blood establishment that they relate to. 
It provides information about internal audits (frequency, date of last audit and outcome of last inspection); 
and audits by regulatory authority (frequency, date of last audit and final outcome of last inspection). 
The suppliers of plasma, for which special criteria are applicable (such as anti-D), should be identified. 
 
The collection and processing operations of the blood establishments and centres should be described and 
summarised. If establishments or centres are no longer used or are temporarily closed, they should be 
listed in a stand-alone table indicating the date when use of the centre or establishment has ceased and the 
reason. 
If there is a separate look back department, the address, duties, and approval status by a recognised 
Competent Authority should be given. 
4.1.2 Characteristics of donations 
 
Specify whether donors are non-remunerated or remunerated. Justify why donors are regarded as voluntary 
and non-remunerated. If applicable, the nature of any compensation for donation should be given. 
4.1.3 Epidemiological data on blood transmissible infections 
 
Should be submitted in accordance with the EMA Guideline on Epidemiological Data on Blood Transmissible 
Infections (EMA/CHMP/BWP/548524/2008)

4.3 Selection or exclusion criteria for blood or plasma donors 
The criteria confirm that each establishment or centre complies with the accepted selection or exclusion 
criteria for blood or plasma donors as per CHMP guidance documents, WHO, and Council of Europe 
recommendations. It is recognised that there may be some challenges with the EU donor selection criteria, 
especially concerning tropical diseases that lead to temporal deferral in Europe. 
Donor selection criteria must be accompanied by a questionnaire that is specific for the countries included. 
Specify any requirements versus emerging infectious agents in a specific country of collection and confirm 
that selection/exclusion criteria for blood/plasma donors complies with such requirements. This should 
include the data of the prevalence/incidence of infectious agents that are present in the country but are not 
screened for by the testing protocol. 
If the epidemiological data of the proposed selection or exclusion criteria suggest that the plasma may pose a 
higher risk of transmitting infectious disease that than of plasma collected in developed countries such as EU, 
USA, UK, Australia and Canada, this should be discussed and the selection criteria for donors justified. The 
upper limits for acceptance of donors based on the prevalence/incidence of transmissible diseases should be 
set and justified. This should, where applicable, be discussed for the country, regions within the country and 
for individual collection centres. 
Indicate whether the plasma from the proposed PMF collection site will be pooled with plasma procured 
from other establishments or whether it will be kept separately. Indicate whether the plasma-derived 
medicinal products will be distributed to all patients served by the manufacturer or only to patients in the 
country of origin of the plasma of the PMF. 
The exclusion criteria for (v)Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease should be stated and comply with EMEA and 
CHMP guidance.

4.4 System in place which enables the path taken by each donation to be traced from  
      the blood or plasma collection establishment through to finished products and  
      vice versa. 
 It summarises the implemented system that enables the path of each donation to be traced from the 
Blood or plasma collection facility through to the finished product. This must include the testing facility, 
identification procedures, labelling and record keeping. 
Give information on steps that would be taken if it was found retrospectively that donation(s) should have 
been excluded from processing (“look-back procedure”) and justify the system in accordance with accepted 
guidelines and procedures. 
4.4.1 Look-back procedure 
 
If a plasma unit fails release due to any of the reasons listed below, a look-back should be instituted. The 
process consists of tracing back and the additional testing of previous donations for at least six months prior 
to the last negative donation. The following procedures should be documented in a standard operating 
procedure: 
 
a) it is found that the donor did not meet the relevant donor health criteria 
b) a subsequent donation from a donor previously found negative for viral markers is found 
positive for any of the viral markers 
c) it is discovered that testing for viral markers has not been carried out according to agreed 
procedures 
d) the donor has developed an infectious disease caused by an agent potentially transmissible 
by plasma-derived products (HBV, HCV, HAV and other non-A, non-B, non-C hepatitis 
viruses, HIV 1 and 2 and other agents in the light of current knowledge) 
e) the donor develops Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease 
f) the recipient of blood or a blood component develops post- transfusion/infusion infection 
which implicates or can be traced back to the donor

6. EPIDEMIOLOGICAL DATA 
 
The data should be reported and evaluated as per the EMA Guideline on the scientific data requirements for 
a plasma master file. 
The systems to monitor changes of epidemiological data must be described. The information should be 
separate for repeat tested (RT) and first-time tested (FTT) donors of source and recovered plasma and given 
for each blood establishment/blood or plasma collection center and country as appropriate. 
There should be an overview of how the epidemiological data of the individual collection centers are 
compared. The in-house alert limits to identify outliers should be given for the FTT and RT donors for source 
and recovered plasma and for donors from other countries. The in-house alert limits should be justified. The 
corrective actions taken if a collection center exceeds alert limits must be given and justified. 
If the epidemiological data of the PMF suggest that the plasma may pose a higher risk of transmitting 
infectious disease that than plasma collected in developed countries such as the EU, USA, UK, Australia and 
Canada, this should be discussed and the use of the plasma of the PMF justified. Plasma with unjustifiable 
higher risk of transmitting infectious diseases must not be used for the manufacture of PDMPs. 
 
6.1 Selection of donors 
 
The donor selection and exclusion criteria should be provided and comply with international accepted 
standards. Donors are voluntary and non-remunerated. (Directive 2002/98/EC of the European Parliament 
and of the Council of 27 January 2003: Setting standards of quality and safety for the collection, testing, 
processing, storage and distribution of human blood and blood components); WHO Blood donor selection: 
guidelines on assessing donor suitability for blood donation. 
6.2  Testing of donations 
 
The testing of donations should comply with international directives (Directive 2002/98/EC of the European 
Parliament and of the Council of 27 January 2003: Setting standards of quality and safety for the collection, 
testing, processing, storage and distribution of human blood and blood components; WHO recommendations 
for the production, control and regulation of human plasma for fractionation, Annex 4, TRS No 941).

NAT Testing of individual donations or testing of minipools should be described and pool sizes justified. The

6.4 Inventory hold and post-collection measures including look back procedure 
 
Demonstrate and discuss traceability of a donation from the site where the blood /plasma is collected 
through to the finished product and vice versa. Traceability systems should be in accord with EU and WHO 
directives. The data and information on traceability for collection centres that have been closed or do not 
deliver plasma anymore should be kept for 30 years. 
The PMF applicant must demonstrate that there is an inventory hold period of 45 days from the date of 
donation to the first step of the manufacturing process. 
6.5 Residual risk estimation 
 
The residual risk posed by donations that contain viruses not detected by the routine screening tests must be 
calculated in accord with internationally accepted methods such as WHO Guidelines on estimation of 
residual risk of HIV, HBV or HCV infections via cellular blood components and plasma, Annex 4, TRS No 1004.
