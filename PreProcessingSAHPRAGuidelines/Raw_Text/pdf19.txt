substances as defined in the Hazardous Substances Act 15 of 1973, referred to in this guideline as radioactive

precedence.

1. Preliminaries
1.1 Purpose
This guideline specifies the basic security conditions for obtaining a transport authority for Group IV hazardous
substances as defined in the Hazardous Substances Act 15 of 1973, referred to in this guidance as radioactive
material. This guidance is provided in support of the Regulations relating to Group IV Hazardous Substances,
issued in terms of section 29 of the Hazardous Substances Act (15 of 1973) and published under Government
Notice R247 in Government Gazette 14596, dated 26 February 1993 (R247) and any subsequent regulations
made pursuant to the Hazardous Substances Act.
This guidance is not intended to relieve an authorised party of the duty to take any additional actions as may
be appropriate and necessary for radiological safety and security to protect the public, patients, radiation
workers and the environment.
1.2 Scope
This guidance applies to all the transport and associated activities of all Category 1, 2, and 3 radioactive
materials. This guidance does not apply to nuclear material (as defined in the Nuclear Energy Act, No. 46 of
1999), except for radioactive sources that incorporate plutonium-239.
This guidance applies to road, air, rail, and maritime transport of radioactive material both domestically and
internationally and must be adhered to by all conveyors, users, and manufacturers.
1.3 Fundamental Obligation
Any person making an application for the transport of radioactive material must comply with this guidance
and the security-related requirements and conditions of the applicable authority as a condition for receiving
authorisation.

The person holding the Authority to Convey is responsible for security during transport.

At a minimum, such training shall include:
(1)
All employees shall be informed at least annually of the importance of effective security measures

(2)
Records shall be kept of such training and information sessions. The records shall include at a

The Authority Holder shall not transfer (hand over) radioactive material to another party unless:

the Authority Holder is authorised to do so by SAHPRA and
(2)
the recipient possesses a valid authority for the radioactive material; and
(3)
the Authority Holder provides the recipient with all relevant technical information to permit the
safe and secure management of the radioactive material.

3.8 Reporting of Radiological Security Events
The authority holder shall include provisions in the Transport Security Plan to report and take action if a
situation involving the loss of control of unauthorised access to actual or attempted theft or sabotage, or
misrouting during transport of radioactive material occurs. The Authority Holder shall include provisions in

(1)
Immediately inform SAHPRA and SAPS Explosives Unit;
(2)
Take appropriate action to remedy the circumstances and to prevent a recurrence of similar

(3)
Investigate the causes, circumstances, and consequences of the event and
(4)
Within seven days, or as required, provide the Regulator with a report on the causes of the event,
its circumstances and consequences, and on the corrective or preventive actions taken or to be
taken.

4. Categorisation and Assignment of Security Level
Radioactive materials shall be assigned a security category of Enhanced Transport Security Level or Basic
Transport Security Level. Packages containing Category 1 or 2 radioactive sources are assigned to the Enhanced
Transport Security Level, and packages containing Category 3 radioactive sources are assigned to the Basic
Transport Security Level. The category of the material is assigned based on the activity of the sources using
the methodology described below:

The D-value is a radionuclide-specific value assigned to radioactive material which, if not under control,
could cause severe deterministic effects for a range of scenarios that include both external exposure
from unshielded radioactive material and inadvertent internal exposure following dispersal (e.g., by
fire or explosion) of the radioactive material. 

The activity of radioactive material (A) in common applications varies over many orders of magnitude.
D-values are therefore used to normalise the range of activities to provide a reference in comparing
risks. This is done by taking the activity A of the source (in TBq) and dividing it by the D-value for the
relevant radionuclide. 

Authority Holders shall categorise their radioactive material in accordance with Annex I of this
document. Authority Holders shall use the A/D ratio as shown in Table 1 for categorising radioactive
sources with an assigned D value as listed in Table 2.

For unsealed material, if the activity is greater than 3000A2, it falls within the Enhanced Transport
Security Level. If below 3000A2, it falls within the Basic Transport Security Level. A2 values can be
found in Table 2 of IAEA Publication Regulations for the Safe Transport of Radioactive Material (No.
SSR-6 Rev. 1)

5. Requirements for Security in the Transport of Radioactive Material
5.1 Prudent Management Practices
Prudent Management Practices should be applied to all shipments of radioactive material regardless of
material category. These practices are the same basic security precautions that might be applied to any cargo
of high value. 
1)
General security provisions
a)
The Authority Holder shall ensure that all persons engaged in the transport of radioactive material
apply security measures commensurate with their responsibilities and the level of threat.
b)
Radioactive material shall be transferred only to authorised Authority Holders.
c)
When radioactive material is temporarily stored at transit sites or makes an expected or unexpected
stop, appropriate security measures shall be applied consistent with the measures applied during use
and storage.
d)
The Authority Holder shall have procedures in place to ensure that, if any package is not delivered to
the intended recipient at the expected time, an inquiry is initiated about the status of that package. If
the package has been lost or stolen or appears to have been tampered with, procedures shall
immediately be initiated to locate and recover the package.
e)
Enclosed lockable vehicles should be used for transportation of radioactive materials.
f)
Radioactive material must be transported in approved packaging and the transport package must be
locked and secured to the vehicle to prevent inadvertent movement during transport.
g)
Transport containers must be locked inside the vehicle and must be positioned at the maximum
distance possible from the passenger compartment.
h)
If an open vehicle is used, the transport container must be permanently fixed to the vehicle unless it
weighs more than 2000 kg.
i)
Utilising carriers with package tracking systems (e.g., bar code system to monitor the status of the
shipment), as appropriate;
j)
Not leaving packages or conveyances unattended for any longer than is absolutely necessary;
SAHPGL-RDN-RN-20_v1

k)
Providing drivers of road conveyances with effective communication capability;
l)
Satellite tracking system on vehicle.
5.2 Basic Transport Security Level Requirements (Categories 1, 2, and 3)
In addition to Prudent Management Practices, the Basic Transport Security Level Requirements shall be applied
to radioactive material that has been evaluated as Category 1, 2, or 3.
5.2.1
Contingency Planning
Contingency plans include predefined sets of actions for response to off-normal situations, such as
unauthorised acts indicative of attempted unauthorised removal or sabotage, including threats thereof
designed to effectively counter such acts. 
Contingency plans must be developed for all shipments of Category 1, 2, and 3 radioactive materials. The
graded approach will be applied such that the level of rigor and detail will be greater for higher category
material, with Category 1 being the highest category and requiring the most detail.
Category 3 Contingency Plans should include emergency contact telephone numbers and basic driver actions
to take if an off-normal event occurs.
Category 2 Contingency Plans should include emergency contact telephone numbers of detailed instructions
on actions to take if an off-normal event occurs. 
Category 1 Contingency Plans should include comprehensive instructions for response to off-normal situations
based on an analysis of all credible hazards and threats which may arise during the shipment.
General notes on developing contingency plans:

All safety incidents have a security component if the shipment is halted.

Contingency plans are a working document containing formats, such as flow-charts and bulleted lists.

The credible hazards and threats may be identified using a standardised checklist.

Identify categories of risks using the checklist, for example:
o
Environmental/Natural
o
Traffic/Routing
o
Medical
o
Mechanical
SAHPGL-RDN-RN-20_v1
Page 17 of 40

30 March 2026
o
Malicious act

Each risk category should have a response framework developed so that an individual contingency plan
does not need to be developed for every conceivable scenario – the basic actions are the same within
each category. If there is a major difference in protocol, then a new plan can be developed (i.e., if
there is severe weather inbound, vs a mudslide that damages the road – the severe weather might
cause a delay in the shipment, while the mudslide might reroute the shipment, but both natural
disasters).

Contingency plans must always support and not hinder first responders, and should never dictate first
responder actions (i.e., do not tell the police what to do, and need to be compatible with the police
response so that they do not slow down or reduce the effectiveness of the police response).

It is acceptable for security and safety contingency plans to be combined in a single document so that
the driver has access to all contingency plans in a single location. 
5.2.2
Trustworthiness Determination
The Authority Holder shall subject persons engaged in the transport of radioactive material to a
trustworthiness determination commensurate with their responsibilities. 
Methods of determining trustworthiness:
1)
Criminal history check conducted by the South African Police Service (SAPS).
2)
Conflict of interest statement, examples might include self-reported disclosures of any outside business
dealings, which could cause a violation of this security guidance or incentives to compromise the security
of radioactive material in transport. This statement should be reviewed and approved by a senior
management official of the Authority Holder. 
3)
Drug and Alcohol testing should be administered at randomised intervals and should ensure that personnel
performing their duties in the transport of radioactive material are not performing their duties while
impaired. 
5.2.3
Shipper and Carrier Credentials
Each crew member of any conveyance transporting radioactive material shall carry means of identification,
such as an official identification document, including an identity document, driver’s license, or passport.

This photo identification must be accompanied by a written authorisation from the crew member’s employer,
with the names of individuals specifically authorized to perform a given transport.
5.2.4
Written Procedures
The Authority Holder shall develop implementation procedures and provide them to crew members. These
procedures shall address the application of security measures which have been required as a condition of the
Transport Authority issued by the regulatory body. 
Response procedures should include basic steps on how to respond to an off-normal incident during transport,
such as safe parking instructions and emergency points of contact. At a minimum, these procedures should
include 24-hour contact information for the Authority Holder’s radiation protection officer, the regulatory
body, and SAPS. Additionally, they may include emergency contact information for the national emergency
centre and emergency services. Emergency services telephone numbers must be obtained from the local
service providers. 
5.2.5
Security Training
The Authority Holder shall ensure that all persons engaged in the transport of radioactive material receive
training, including training in the elements of security awareness. Such training shall be provided or verified
upon employment and periodically supplemented by retraining. Records of all security training undertaken
shall be kept by the Authority Holder and shall be made available, upon request, to the employee and to the
Regulatory Authority.
Security Awareness training should include the following elements:
a)
Describe the need for transport security;
b)
The nature of security-related threats;
c)

Exchange of security-related information

5.3 Enhanced Transport Security Level Requirements (Category 1 and 2)
In addition to Prudent Management Practices and Basic Transport Security Level Requirements, Enhanced
Transport Security Level Requirements shall be applied to radioactive material which has been evaluated as
Category 1 or 2. 
5.3.1
Information Protection
Measures should be taken to protect sensitive information relating to transport operations, including detailed
information on the schedule and route. Such information should be shared on a need-to-know basis, and
includes the security system design and operation, response capability, detection, assessment, and delay
capabilities. 
In addition, computer security is critical to protecting sensitive information. Computers should be access-
controlled commensurate to the level of information stored. These systems should be accessible only by
persons on a need-to-know basis who have undergone a trustworthiness verification. The primary application
for information protection is found in the security plan, which contains the above-mentioned details.
5.3.2

A Transport Security Plan is required to be submitted to SAHPRA for all Category 1 and 2 transport applications.
The Transport Security Plan for category 2 sources must be reviewed annually.
If any aspect of the transport is subcontracted, the Authority Holder shall ensure that the subcontractor
complies with the Transport Security Plan. The Transport Security Plan shall include at least the following
elements and shall be modified as needed to reflect changes in the threat level:

d)
Clear statements of transport security measures, including training and policies, response to
conditions of a higher-level threat, verification of new employees and employment, operating
practices, equipment, and other resources;

5.3.3
Planning and Coordination
The shipper shall provide advance notification to the receiver and SAHPRA of each planned shipment. This
advance notification must include details of the mode of transport and the expected shipment schedule. The
Consignee shall confirm capability and readiness to accept delivery at the expected time and shall notify the
shipper and SAHPRA on receipt or non-receipt within the expected delivery time frame.
In the case of routine industrial radiography camera use, or other routine Category 2 users, the details of the
transport must be recorded in the source log and be made available to SAHPRA for inspection. No additional
notifications are required. This does not apply in cases where a radiography camera is being shipped for the
purpose of replacing the source and a notification is required.
5.3.4
Physical Security
The physical security system includes those measures that provide detection, delay, and response.

5.3.4.1 Detection
At a minimum, detection is provided by the vehicle crew who have been briefed on the Transport Security
Plan and have been provided with emergency contact information. The vehicle crew should be instructed to
immediately contact law enforcement as well as their RPO if any suspicious activity is observed.
NSS 9-G Section 5.48. Electronic intrusion detection and alarms, including duress alarms, should be considered.
Electronic intrusion detection technologies can be suitable for providing an immediate indication of an
intrusion into the cargo area. Examples of this technology include the following:
(a) Balanced magnetic door switches;
(b) Light sensors (for closed conveyances);
(c) Fibre optic and other electronic seals;
(d) Passive infrared, microwave or video motion detection.
If electronic intrusion detection systems are implemented, they must be remotely monitored by personnel
with training on the correct procedures to follow if an alarm is detected. 
Enhanced detection will be provided by armed escorts and the RPO accompanying the shipment. Each must
be able to communicate with SAHPRA, SAPS, and the Emergency Centre.
5.3.4.2 Delay
Locks and seals should be applied to conveyances, compartments or freight containers. Locks and seals should
be checked to confirm the integrity before dispatch, after any stops made during the journey and during any
intermodal transfer of each radioactive material consignment. 
If enclosed freight containers are used, verification of the integrity of a door seal is sufficient, and individual
seals on packages inside the freight container do not need to be verified. Lock fittings and components, such
as attachment points and tie-downs, should be complementary to the quality and strength of the locks. In
cases where chains are unavailable, the package should be contained within an ISO Intermodal Container to
provide an additional barrier against direct access to the package.
Procedures should be established to ensure the security of keys to conveyances and locks.

5.3.4.3 Response
Response is provided by SAPS per their operating procedures. The Authority Holder must include contact
information for SAPS in the instructions provided to the crew of the vehicle. 
5.3.5
Communications
Routine communications may be conducted by mobile telephone. Techniques such as code words and phrases
should be used to reduce the amount of sensitive information that is shared over open communications. This
does not apply in an emergency.

The Consignor and Conveyor should conduct a pre-shipment security verification of the conveyance and
security systems before commencing transport. The purpose of this verification is to ensure that the security
measures are implemented as described in the Transport Security Plan and are functioning normally.
This verification should ensure that security measures specified in the Prudent Management Practices, Basic
Transport Security Level, and Enhanced Transport Security Level have been implemented as a condition for
receiving a Transport Authority.

5.4 Other Security Measures for Category 1 Shipments
In addition to Prudent Management Practices, Basic Transport Security Level Requirements, and Enhanced
Transport Security Level Requirements, the following provisions shall be applied to radioactive material that
has been evaluated as Category 1.
5.4.1
Advance Notification
The Consignor shall provide advance notification to the receiver of each planned shipment, mode of transport,
and expected delivery time. The Consignee shall confirm capability and readiness to accept delivery at the
expected time and shall notify the Consignor on receipt or non-receipt within the expected delivery time frame.

5.4.2
Shipment Monitoring and Tracking
The Authority Holder shall use tracking methods or devices to monitor the movement of packages or
conveyances containing radioactive material. All packages shall be tracked during transport to ensure that the
status of the package can be determined at any time. Road vehicles transporting Category 1 quantities of
radioactive material shall be equipped with automatic and real-time tracking capability.
5.4.3
Additional Physical Security
Enhanced detection will be provided by armed escorts, the driver, and the radiation protection officer
accompanying the shipment. Each will have a method for communication with SAHPRA, SAPS, and the National
Emergency Centre.
Enhanced delay will be provided by chain tie-downs/any other tie-down that does not compromise the
integrity of the transport container. In cases where chains are not available, the package should be contained
within an ISO Intermodal Container to provide an additional barrier against direct access to the package. 
Enhanced response will be provided by armed escorts who will be responsible for the interruption and
neutralisation of a security incident according to their own operating procedures.  
5.4.4
Additional Pre-Shipment Verification
Category 1 pre-shipment security verification should be performed in stages. The first stage should occur well
in advance of shipments to ensure deficiencies are identified and time is available to resolve them. Final
verification should be conducted using a checklist just prior to departure to ensure that all security measures
included in the Transport Security Plan are in place and operational. This pre-shipment verification should be
documented using a signed and dated checklist before shipment departure.

BOX I–2. DETAILS TO BE CONSIDERED FOR THE TRANSPORT SECURITY PLAN (cont.)
1. EMERGENCY  RESPONSE
Emergency response includes both tactical and non-tactical (i.e., non-security-related emergency)
planning. This section identifies the range of incidents that might need response measures to be
initiated, describes the appropriate response measures, and clearly defines the response resources.
1.1. Emergency and contingency response
This subsection identifies handling responses of non-nuclear or non-radiological emergency
and security-related incidents; it is not to be confused with specific arrangements for response to a
nuclear or radiological emergency. Response actions to be included in this subsection are actions
that will be taken by crew members, the transport control centre or other operations centres;
shipper and receiver technical support staff; emergency response units along the route; escort
personnel (if present); guard or security force (if present), and response forces.
Emergency situations can include road closure, vehicle breakdown, vehicle accidents, and driver
illness. Corresponding emergency arrangements could include the availability of backup vehicles and
drivers, capabilities for towing and lifting, and plans for use of safe havens.
This subsection describes any necessary advance information to respond to forces along the route,
including the time within which the communication of information must be completed before the
shipment. It also identifies any accompanying guard or security force.
1.2. Communications  during  incidents
This subsection includes a description of the communications systems and actions that will be taken
to address both emergency and nuclear security events. This information could include the types of
communications equipment used and features to ensure the security of communications.
1.3. Reporting of threats and incidents
This subsection describes reporting requirements, including types of events that must be reported,  
to  whom  and  how  the  event  will  be  reported,  and  the timeframe for reporting.
